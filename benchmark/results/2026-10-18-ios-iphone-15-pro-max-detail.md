# iOS, iPhone 15 Pro Max, opening a photo while the grid loads, 2026-10-18

`run.ts --subjects fast-image-local,image,expo-image,nitro-image,turbo-image --scenarios detail --metrics ''`: the benchmark app on Expo SDK 57 (React Native 0.86.3, New Architecture), Release, 5 runs per library, on an iPhone 15 Pro Max (iOS 27.0) over USB. FastImage 10.0 (FastImage (this checkout)), React Native Image (React Native 0.86.3), expo-image 57.0.5, Nitro Image 0.15.2, Turbo Image 1.24.3, each with its defaults and without fades. Images served on the phone by the XCUITest runner, with 40 ms latency and no bandwidth limit. The libraries ran one after another, in this order.

`detail` as in the reference runs of 2026-10-12 (the grid's 60 photos, and 100 ms later the last of them, the one whose download the grid asked for last, at the screen's width over the grid, which goes on loading under it), run again with `priority: 'high'` for Nitro Image too: its url source's `options.priority`, which the benchmark's adapter didn't set before. So FastImage, expo-image and Nitro Image ask for high priority; React Native Image and Turbo Image have none. Only that photo is timed: its first image is its last.

Nitro Image with `priority: 'high'` showed the photo at 548 ms (532–572), as without it (548 ms on 2026-10-12): its priority becomes SDWebImage's high priority, but the grid's download of the photo is already queued, and SDWebImage reuses it without raising its priority (as for FastImage and expo-image).

Times in ms from the start of the run (the frame its clock starts in, as the app renders the subject's views), from screen recordings, timed by the clock the app draws in each frame: median / p90 over the runs (with 5 runs, p90 is the slowest).
